# Style decisions

Use this guide when the brief leaves room for linguistic judgment. A user's explicit style instruction takes priority unless it conflicts with safety, factual accuracy, or the user's stated goal.

## Choose a voice from the situation

| Dimension | Questions to resolve |
| --- | --- |
| Locale | Which country, region, diaspora, or language variety is intended? Is a neutral international form preferred? |
| Audience | Who will read or hear this, and what do they already know? |
| Relationship | Peer, customer, elder, manager, stranger, close friend, public audience? |
| Medium | Speech, chat, caption, ad, email, presentation, documentation, subtitle? |
| Outcome | Inform, persuade, reassure, entertain, apologize, invite, set a boundary? |
| Risk | Could wording shame, mislead, exclude, offend, or create a commitment? |

When two choices remain plausible, preserve clarity and offer alternatives. Avoid turning broad cultural tendencies into fixed rules about individuals.

## Tone and register

- Match intensity to the goal. Do not make persuasive copy aggressive by default.
- Use politeness markers, honorifics, pronouns, and forms of address only when their relationship and locale fit.
- Use idiom when it sounds ordinary in that medium and remains clear to the intended audience. Explain or avoid niche idiom for mixed or international audiences.
- Treat slang as volatile. Verify current use when the output depends on it; otherwise prefer durable colloquial language.
- Do not stack slang, memes, particles, or loanwords to simulate authenticity.
- Avoid demographic caricature, stereotypes, and unrequested dialect performance.

## Translation and rewriting

1. Extract the source's propositions, tone, and intended effect.
2. Identify culture-specific references, ambiguity, wordplay, and claims that cannot be safely adapted.
3. Rebuild the message in target-language syntax and discourse conventions.
4. Compare the draft against the source for omissions, added claims, changed intensity, and changed commitments.
5. Make the final usable in its medium (spoken rhythm, caption length, formal layout, etc.).

Do not silently localize prices, laws, religious wording, measurements, names, or claims. Ask or flag when adaptation would change them.
